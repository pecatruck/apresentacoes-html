# Product

## Register

Brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Professionals in the Brazilian heavy-vehicle aftermarket: repair shops, parts-counter teams, fleet operators, distributors, and other people responsible for diagnosing, selecting, applying, selling, and supporting cabin shock absorbers.

## Product Purpose

Support a two-hour, presenter-led workshop that teaches better diagnosis, correct application, and more confident selling of cabin shock absorbers. The deck should reduce rushed diagnosis and prevent returns by connecting technical reasoning, process evidence, traceability, commercial value, and the TechShocks brand story.

## Brand Personality

Technical, direct, and trustworthy. The voice is confident without exaggeration, accessible to a mixed technical and commercial audience, and explicit about evidence, limitations, and information that still requires validation.

## Anti-references

- Generic corporate or SaaS presentation aesthetics.
- Dense catalogue-like layouts that try to show everything at once.
- Visually polluted compositions with insufficient breathing room.
- Decorative or gimmicky animation that competes with the presenter.
- Unsupported technical or commercial claims presented as established facts.
- Small text, weak contrast, and layouts designed for close reading rather than projection.

## Design Principles

1. Technical clarity comes before advertising: explain the reasoning, process, and limits before making a brand claim.
2. Trust must be provable: distinguish evidence, validation status, and pending information visibly and honestly.
3. Design for a live presentation rhythm: use strong hierarchy, deliberate sequencing, short reveal animations, and pauses that support the speaker.
4. Optimize for distance: keep text concise, contrast high, margins consistent, and focal points unmistakable on a projected 16:9 canvas.
5. Preserve the TechShocks visual language: orange, black, graphite, and white; industrial typography; technical linework; angular frames; restrained depth and texture.
6. Keep slides focused: each screen should communicate one dominant idea and use secondary detail only when it supports that idea.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG AA contrast where practical, with additional emphasis on projection conditions.
- Maintain high legibility at distance and avoid relying on fine detail or small labels for essential meaning.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and present the final state immediately when motion is reduced.
- Never communicate status, sequence, or meaning through color alone; pair color with labels, icons, numbering, or structure.
- Keep interactions operable by keyboard, pointer, touch, and wheel navigation.
- Use clear Brazilian Portuguese and avoid unnecessary jargon for the mixed technical and commercial audience.
